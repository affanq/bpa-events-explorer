Description & Eligibility
Teams will design and develop an original endless -runner video game that performs and behaves as
outlined in the provided topic. Project submissions will consist of game source code, art and audio assets,
a Game Manual, and a functional, executable version of the game. This national event will be submitted
and judged virtually. Awards will be presented at the National Leadership Conference. Any middle level,
secondary or postsecondary contestant may enter this event. A team will consist of 2
-4 contestants. There
are no restrictions on the number of entries per chapter or per state. Teams who do not submit an entry
following the topic listed below or do not follow the Copyright and/or Fair Use Guidelines will be
disqualified.

Topic
Afterburn — Endless Runner . Create an endless runner game titled "Afterburn." Players control a young
inventor riding a self -built rocket-powered skateboard through a sprawling futuristic city after an
experimental energy surge sends the board into overdrive. As malfunctioning robots
, collapsing
scaffolding, and rogue delivery drones flood the streets, players must dash, jump, and slide through
procedurally generated urban obstacles — collecting scattered power crystals to keep the board running
and unlocking new gadgets along the way . Each run generates a unique city route, ensuring no two
attempts are the same.

Contest Specifications for this Event

• Original endless -runner gameplay built around the topic, featuring continuous forward motion and
procedurally generated routes
• Core player actions include dash, jump, and slide, plus at least one gadget or boost mechanic
earned through progression
• A menu interface for non -gameplay interaction (e.g., starting a run, resuming, exiting, viewing high
scores, viewing/equipping unlocked gadgets)
• An on-screen HUD displaying vital gameplay information (e.g., distance, score, fuel/energy, active
gadget)
• A minimum average framerate of 30 frames per second (FPS) during typical play

• Persistent storage of player progression data (high scores, unlocked gadgets, settings) saved and
loaded between sessions
• Optional databases or persistence options include local file storage (JSON, XML, SQLite), Oracle
Express, Microsoft SQL Server, Oracle MySQL, PostgreSQL, Google Firebase, or Amazon Web
Services. Local persistence is acceptable
• Languages/frameworks permitted include Java, JavaScript, Python, Ruby, Microsoft C#, Objective
-
C/Cocoa, C++, Apple Swift, Unity, Unreal Engine, Godot, GameMaker, and other freely available
game engines or libraries
• Any submission using a game development framework must clearly show the extent of original
work done via source code comments
• Use of integrated development environments (e.g., Microsoft Visual Studio, Eclipse, Oracle
NetBeans, or Apple XCode) is encouraged
