Topic
Student Athlete Offered Money to Promote a Product She Doesn't Use.
Devon is a junior distance runner with
a growing following online, mostly other high school athletes who watch her training videos and race
breakdowns. She is careful about what she posts and proud that people trust her.

A recovery drink company reaches out with an offer: four thousand dollars ($4,000) for six posts across the
season. The money would cover her club fees, travel to two out
-of-state meets, and a chunk of what her family
has been putting on a credit card.

Devon tries the drink. She does not like it, and more importantly, she does not think it does anything. She
already uses a cheaper product that works fine for her.

The company's contract does not require her to say she uses the drink daily. It requires six posts featuring the
product, tagged and disclosed as sponsored. Everything about it is legal and above board.

Devon's coach says every athlete she knows takes these deals, and that a sponsorship post is understood by
everyone to be an advertisement, not a personal recommendation. Her teammate points out that Devon's
audience is mostly other teenagers who look up t o her and might buy something they cannot really afford
because Devon appeared to endorse it.

Devon's mother has not said much, except that the credit card balance is real.

The company needs an answer by Friday.

Consider the following:
• Should Devon accept the offer?
• Does a sponsorship disclosure fully solve the honesty problem, or only part of it?

• Does it change your answer that her audience is mostly teenagers?
• Is there a version of this deal Devon could accept in good conscience?

Contest Competencies

1.
Demonstrate integrity and accountability by adhering consistently to ethical principles, acting with
honesty and fairness, and taking responsibility for the decisions and consequences that result from
those actions
2.
Apply transparent communication practices by clearly articulating the rationale behind decisions,
developing and conveying an ethical vision, and ensuring stakeholders are informed and aligned

3.
Demonstrate empathy by considering the perspectives, feelings, and needs of others in the decision
-
making process, treating all individuals with compassion and respect

4.
Apply objectivity by grounding decisions in facts, evidence, and rational analysis, actively avoiding
personal bias, favoritism, and self -interest
5.
Demonstrate courage by making and defending difficult decisions that uphold ethical principles, even
in the face of opposition, pressure, or potential negative consequences

6.
Promote collaboration and inclusion by engaging stakeholders, seeking diverse perspectives, and
fostering a participatory decision -making process that leads to fair and well -informed outcomes
7. Demonstrate adaptability and a commitment to continuous learning by remaining open to new
information, feedback, and emerging ethical trends, and adjusting decisions when necessary to uphold
ethical standards
8. Demonstrate ethical awareness by recognizing when a decision carries ethical weight, identifying
concerns embedded in problems framed as technical, operational, or financial, and surfacing them
before they become consequential
9. Apply legal and regulatory literacy by identifying the laws, disclosure requirements, and professional
standards relevant to a decision, and by complying with their intent rather than only their letter

10. Apply stakeholder and consequence analysis by identifying all parties affected by a decision,
including those absent from it, and by evaluating both immediate and long -term impacts.
