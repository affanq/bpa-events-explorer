Description & Eligibility
Teams will create a website based on the assigned topic. Any middle level contestant may enter this judged
team event. A team will consist of 2 -4 contestants. Teams must be registered for the national competition
before the submission deadline and compete in both parts of the event to be ranked.
Entries that do not
follow the provided topic or the Copyright and/or Fair Use Guidelines or Artificial Intelligence Usage &
Citation Policy will be disqualified.

Topic
Create a website that celebrates unsung heroes in your local community
— everyday people who make a
meaningful difference. This could include teachers, coaches, first responders, volunteers, or neighbors who go
above and beyond. Your website must include a landing page and at least three (3) of the following sub -pages:
• Hero Spotlight — A featured profile page with a biography and photo
• Why They Matter — A page explaining the impact of their contributions
• Community Impact — Stories or data showing how the community has benefited
• How to Get Involved — Ways students can volunteer or support similar causes
• Nomination Page — A form or process for nominating a local hero
• Photo Gallery — Images from community events or service activities
• Interview / Q&A — A page featuring quotes or responses from the featured hero

• Resources & Links — Connections to local organizations or volunteer opportunities

Team Must Supply
• Students may use one single -sided laptop, notebook, or tablet for their presentation. Projection
equipment and external displays will not be provided or permitted.

Contest Notes
• Method of evaluation: judged
• AI Policy Level 2 Permitted with Citation
• Carry-in and setup of equipment must be done solely by the team and must take place within the time
allotted
• No Internet access will be provided on -site at NLC; however, teams may provide their own access to
be used only for their presentation to the judges
• Length of event : no more than three (3) minutes for setup, no more than five (5) minutes for the
presentation, no more than five (5) minutes for judges’ questions
• Finals may be part of the competition
• Each state is allowed three (3) entries
